Applied AI research

Computer vision in the field and lab

Two studies at Truman State University explored how computer vision can speed up road inspections and automate microplastic counting.

Truman State University · Sep–Nov 2026

AI-powered pothole detection using drone imagery

Developed a computer vision workflow to detect potholes in drone imagery and map their locations using GPS.

28.2%Less inspection time in the pilot
19.5 → 14 minManual vs. AI-assisted inspection

I trained a YOLO11n model using Python, PyTorch, and Roboflow, analyzed detection errors, incorporated human verification, and mapped verified potholes using GPS. The pilot compared the AI-assisted workflow with two independent manual inspections.

AI-powered microplastic detection

Automated microplastic detection and counting using low-cost microscopy. The workflow spans image acquisition, CVAT annotation, PyTorch-based YOLO11 training, and evaluation.

Data preparation

Microscope image annotation

200 annotated microscope images

Acquired microscope images and annotated microplastic particles in CVAT to prepare a dataset for YOLO11 object detection.

Model evaluation

Automated detection and counting

95.0% mAP50 · 90.3% precision · 93.2% recall

Trained and evaluated YOLO11 as part of an end-to-end pipeline, from image acquisition to automated particle detection and counting.

Validation

Comparison with human counts

Three independent human observers

Achieved a Pearson correlation of 0.997 with human-reference counts, with 100% of predictions within ±1 particle of the human reference.
